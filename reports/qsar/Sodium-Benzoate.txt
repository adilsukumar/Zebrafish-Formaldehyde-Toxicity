3. RELEVANCE TO AQUATIC TOXICITY
Sodium benzoate is an ionic, highly water-soluble solid used widely as a preservative. Its high water solubility and very low estimated log Kow indicate a strong preference for the aqueous phase rather than hydrophobic phases. These physicochemical properties are relevant when interpreting its aquatic toxicity because the compound is readily present in the dissolved phase.

4. Working Interpretation
Sodium benzoate has a recorded Danio rerio mortality LC50 of 461 mg/L after 48 h. The selected QSAR Toolbox profiling results assign a Basesurface narcotics mode of action for acute aquatic toxicity. BioWin predicts fast biodegradation, with ultimate biodegradation occurring over weeks, while the bioaccumulation metabolism half-life is classified as very fast. The Cramer profiler gives a Low (Class I) hazard classification.
Note: Exposure duration is retained exactly as reported in the database; the 48 h value is not treated as a 96 h value.
