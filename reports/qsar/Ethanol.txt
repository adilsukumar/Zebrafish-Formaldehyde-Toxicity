3. RELEVANCE TO AQUATIC TOXICITY
Ethanol is a small, highly water-miscible organic liquid with a low boiling point and high volatility compared with the other chemicals in this experiment. Its very high water solubility and low log Kow indicate limited tendency for partitioning into non-polar phases. The high flammability is an important handling consideration. These physicochemical properties are relevant when interpreting aquatic toxicity because ethanol readily disperses in water and has comparatively low hydrophobicity.

4. Working Interpretation
Ethanol has a recorded Danio rerio mortality LC50 of 1.56 × 10^4 mg/L after 120 h (5 days). The selected QSAR Toolbox profiling results classify ethanol as a neutral organic and assign a base-surface narcotic mode of action for acute aquatic toxicity. The Cramer profiler gives Low (Class I) hazard classification, while the selected genotoxicity/carcinogenicity alert profilers report no alert found.
Note: Exposure duration is retained exactly as reported in the database; the 120 h value is not treated as a 96 h value.
